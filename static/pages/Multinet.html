Research

A generalization benchmark for next-generation multimodal models.

MultiNet is an open research program — by Fig and Manifold Research — building benchmarks that measure whether multimodal models truly generalize across vision, language, and action, rather than excelling only on the narrow tasks they were trained for.

Why

A systematic benchmark for the age of action-grounded AI.

Recent advances have shown large-scale models generalizing across diverse tasks. Vision-Language-Action models like π0 take vision-language-grounded actions in robotics, yet are typically specialized to narrow control tasks — and their broader understanding stays underexplored. Vision-Language models like GPT-5 reason impressively over language and images, yet their behavior on genuinely out-of-distribution tasks — low-level robotics control, discrete-action gameplay — is rarely quantified. To build generalist systems that complete long-horizon tasks and bring real value, we first need to understand and measure what these systems can actually do.

MultiNet addresses these gaps by:

Advancing generalist models

A comprehensive benchmark evaluating today's generalist models across multiple modalities, domains, and environments.

Enabling the next generation

Precise insight into failure modes and capabilities on out-of-distribution tasks — informing models that can reach SoTA across all constituent tasks.

Standardizing evaluation

Unified assessment protocols for robotics foundation models beyond narrow control, and vision-language models beyond understanding.

Democratizing access

Open-source tooling that fixes outdated formats, poor maintenance, and accessibility issues across rich but neglected evaluation datasets.

What's next

Where the research is heading.

MultiNet is a step toward next-generation AI systems. The directions we're pursuing from here:

  • Causal world-model evaluation. Leverage world models as evaluators to test whether systems understand causal relationships rather than statistical correlations — enabling counterfactual analysis through systematic environmental perturbation. (The direct seed of v2.0.)
  • Live, dynamic benchmarks. Continuously updating evaluations that adapt to model capability, preventing overfitting to static test sets while assessing deployed systems in real time.
  • Cross-modal failure analysis. Understand why models misalign input domains and output modalities, and design modifications that prevent cross-modal contamination in multi-task settings.
  • Progressive multi-domain training. Learning paradigms that add domains without catastrophic forgetting — moving beyond zero-shot toward rapid, knowledge-preserving adaptation.
